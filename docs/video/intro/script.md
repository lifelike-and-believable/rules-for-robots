## What changed from draft 1, from the maintainer's notes

- **Beat 1: no outline of the house.** "The outline of the house is confusing, and doesn't really contribute. ... The ask is clear enough without it." The dashed outline is gone from the whole film. Beat 1 shows the hill and the round tree, which is what later makes "Why is it a pine tree?" land. The only drawing of the idea is the pencil sketch in beat 8.
- **Beat 3: the agent takes no liberties.** "The agent isn't taking liberties, it just was never told that keeping the tree the same was important." "Plus a few improvements" is gone. The agent says "Added! One ladder.", in the same voice as "Done! One treehouse."
- **Beat 4: the slide lands on the ladder.** The slide is set on top of the ladder and blocks it, in place of going through it. Your line becomes "It's blocking the ladder."
- **Beat 5: micromanaging, not rules.** The heading is "So you micromanage." and your order is "Ask before you change anything.", which the maintainer kept because "it makes it explicit". It is now something you say, not a sticky note, and "KEEP THE TREE." is gone. This also removes the contradiction the review found between mocking rules and the name.
- **Beat 6: the agent remembers nothing.** "Slide tower" and the agent's unprompted suggestion are gone. You ask to continue, the agent asks what you are working on, you say the treehouse, and it adds another box to the tree with the same "Done! One treehouse." as beat 2.

Decided earlier: the master runs about 60 seconds ("60 seconds sounds good"), and a 30-second cut is made at stage 10.

## For the maintainer to decide

1. **Length.** This draft runs 64 seconds, because beats 5 and 6 gained lines. Dropping "May I use the hammer?" from beat 5 brings it to about 62. The session recommends keeping all three questions: the pile-up is the joke, and the end card answers the third.
2. **Beat 4's last line.** "It's blocking the ladder." is the session's wording.
3. **Beat 5's order as a bubble.** "Ask before you change anything." is now your speech, not a sticky note. Say if the note is wanted back.
4. **Beat 6's heading.** "The next morning, it remembers nothing." replaces "a new session has to guess."
5. **The turn's second line.** "Rules for Robots teaches your agent to be one." is the session's wording.
6. **The end card's plain line.** "Rules and plugins for Claude Code." names one agent. If the volumes are meant for more than Claude Code, say what it should name.
7. **The order of beats 8 and 9.** The session recommends the order above: the sketch has to be approved before there is a flagpole to choose a flag for.
