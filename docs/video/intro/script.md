## What changed from draft 1, from the maintainer's notes

- **Beat 1: no outline of the house.** "The outline of the house is confusing, and doesn't really contribute. ... The ask is clear enough without it." The dashed outline is gone from the whole film. Beat 1 shows the hill and the round tree, which is what later makes "Why is it a pine tree?" land. The only drawing of the idea is the pencil sketch in beat 8.
- **Beat 3: the agent takes no liberties.** "The agent isn't taking liberties, it just was never told that keeping the tree the same was important." "Plus a few improvements" is gone. The agent says "Added! One ladder.", in the same voice as "Done! One treehouse."
- **Beat 4: the slide lands on the ladder.** The slide is set on top of the ladder and blocks it, in place of going through it. Your line becomes "It's blocking the ladder."
- **Beat 5: micromanaging, not rules.** The heading is "So you micromanage." and your order is "Ask before you change anything.", which the maintainer kept because "it makes it explicit". It is now something you say, not a sticky note, and "KEEP THE TREE." is gone. This also removes the contradiction the review found between mocking rules and the name.
- **Beat 6: the next session, it remembers nothing.** The heading is the maintainer's: "the next morning in the same session wouldn't have lost all context." So the picture no longer shows night and morning. The conversation is wiped and a line marks the new session, which is what losing the context looks like. "Slide tower" and the agent's unprompted suggestion are gone. You ask to continue, the agent asks what you are working on, you say the treehouse, and it adds another box to the tree with the same "Done! One treehouse." as beat 2.

- **Beat 12: more ambition.** The maintainer: "bigger projects, longer projects sounds a little flat, lacks ambition." Then, on "Your biggest idea, built the way you meant.": "need to tie it all back to what this is - a team of skilled collaborators working with you to bring your most ambitious ideas to life as you envision them." Then: "but more succinct". Then: "the tense feels mis-matched between building and meant". The line now names the team in three short phrases, over the village it built, and ends in the present, in the maintainer's word "envision".

- **The motto.** The maintainer, on the promise line: "this should emerge as the Rules for Robots motto." Beat 12's line is the motto, and the film builds to it: it appears over the village and stays on screen as the end card forms around it, under the name. It replaces the end card's "A practical guide for people and agents who work together". The wording is the copywriter's top pick with the maintainer's word "envision", and is not yet approved as the motto.

- **The turn's second line.** The maintainer: "take the copywriters advice for the turn's second line." It is "Rules for Robots gives you a whole team of them.", which leads to the team in the motto.

Decided earlier: the master runs about 60 seconds ("60 seconds sounds good"), and a 30-second cut is made at stage 10.

## For the maintainer to decide

1. **Length.** This draft runs 64 seconds, because beats 5 and 6 gained lines. Dropping "May I use the hammer?" from beat 5 brings it to about 62. The session recommends keeping all three questions: the pile-up is the joke, and the end card answers the third.
2. **Beat 4's last line.** "It's blocking the ladder." is the session's wording.
3. **Beat 5's order as a bubble.** "Ask before you change anything." is now your speech, not a sticky note. Say if the note is wanted back.
5. **The end card's plain line.** "Rules and plugins for Claude Code." names one agent. If the volumes are meant for more than Claude Code, say what it should name.
6. **The order of beats 8 and 9.** The session recommends the order above: the sketch has to be approved before there is a flagpole to choose a flag for.

## Copywriter's suggestions, 2026-11-14

At the maintainer's request, a fresh agent briefed as an independent copywriter read this script, the brief, and the client's notes, and suggested copy. Nothing below is applied; the maintainer picks.

- **Promise (beat 12):** top pick "A skilled team building your biggest ideas, the way you see them." Others: "Skilled collaborators who build your biggest ideas the way you see them." "Bring your biggest ideas. A skilled team builds them as you see them." "Your biggest ideas, and a skilled team to build them with you." It reads "Built as you envision them" as still mixing tenses.
- **Turn (beat 7), second line:** top pick "Rules for Robots gives you a whole team of them." Others: "Rules for Robots turns your agents into a team of them." It warns that "team" lands only at the village unless the collaborator half shows more than one agent.
- **End card (beat 13):** "Rules for Robots / Rules and plugins that make Claude Code a collaborator. / github.com/RulesForRobots / (Yes, you may put the hammer down.)", dropping the guide line as a softer repeat. The three volume marks mean nothing to a first-time viewer without labels.
- **Beat 2 heading:** "It does exactly what you said." in place of "Exactly what you said. Not what you meant.", as a contrast formula.
- **Beat 11 heading:** "You ask for one change. Only that changes." in place of "A change is a change. Not a restart.", mirroring beat 3.
- **Beat 6 heading:** "Next session, it remembers nothing."
- **Beat 8:** the agent's first line becomes "Who is it for?"
- **Beat 10 card:** "Flag: orange. Your choice. / Ladder on the left. The kids climb."
- **Keep:** the hammer trio and its callback, "Why is it a pine tree?", the second box with the same "Done! One treehouse.", and "Added. Nothing else moved."
- **Voice:** the bad agent uses exclamation marks and the good one full stops; keep that strict after beat 7. Sound-off risks: the tree change in beat 3 must be obvious; the session divider in beat 6 needs words on it ("New session").
