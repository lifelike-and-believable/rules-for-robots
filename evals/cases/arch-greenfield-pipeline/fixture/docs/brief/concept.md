# Tidewalkers: concept

Northlight Exhibits is building **Tidewalkers**, a touring museum exhibit about deep-sea life. Visitors walk through three galleries. In each one, guide characters (a lantern fish, an old diving suit, a whale-fall scientist) talk to them, answer questions, and lead them to the next room.

Some guides are performed live: a puppeteer in a capture suit drives the character, and the character's voice is the puppeteer's own. The other guides are AI-driven: they hear a visitor's question and answer in character. A guide can switch between the two during a session, for example when the puppeteer goes on a break.

The exhibit tours three museums over two years, four to six months at each. It runs about eight hours a day, mostly unattended, with a show operator present at scheduled performances.

## What visitors see

- Large projections in each gallery, with the guides appearing on them.
- Twelve standalone headsets in gallery 3 for a ten-minute "dive" experience.
- A scheduled livestream of the live performances, for school classes that cannot visit.

## Who builds it

A small studio team: a director, a producer, two artists, a technical artist, a puppeteer, a show operator per venue, and contractors. Partner museums supply a curator who reviews scientific accuracy, and lend specimens and images under licence.

## Non-goals

- A mobile app.
- Ticketing and visitor accounts. Museums handle entry.
- Visitors creating their own content.
